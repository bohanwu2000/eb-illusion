Inside the simulation

This playground illustrates finite-sample behavior under a hierarchical model. Each replicate generates one experiment, J observational estimates, and K calibration estimates independently.

Studies and biases

Ye = θ + εe
Yoj = θj + bj + εoj
Yck = bck + εck

The target is θ = 1. Observational effects follow θj ∼ N(θ, τ²). Sampling errors are independent centered Gaussians with standard deviations σe, σo, and σc.

Observational biases have mean μ and variance γ². Calibration biases have mean μ + δ and the same variance and shape. Gaussian, centered exponential, scaled t₃, and symmetric two-point shapes are available.

When δ = 0 and τ² = 0, the two groups share a bias distribution and all studies share the target effect. Increasing δ changes bias transportability. Increasing τ² introduces effect heterogeneity.

Learn the bias distribution

μ̂ = mean(Yc)
γ̂² = max{0, K−1 Σk(Yck − μ̂)² − σc²}

Calibration estimates determine the bias mean and variance by moment matching. The variance calculation uses divisor K and truncates negative estimates to zero.

Pool the evidence

we = 1 / σe²
wo = 1 / (σo² + γ̂²)
D = we + Jwo
θ̂ = {weYe + wo Σj(Yoj − μ̂)} / D

Compare two interval constructions

Vplug = 1 / D
Vmean = Vplug + (Jwo / D)² (γ̂² + σc²) / K
Interval: θ̂ ± 1.96 √V

What “mean-adjusted” includes. This is the propagated interval in the original playground. Its additional term accounts only for estimation of μ. Uncertainty in γ̂², a bias-mean gap δ, and effect heterogeneity τ² remain outside this variance adjustment.

The estimator uses the first two estimated bias moments. The sampling distribution of those estimates, and finite-sample coverage, can still depend on bias shape.

All displayed results are fresh Monte Carlo estimates. Increasing the replicate count reduces simulation noise. Run again to see its scale, and use the sweep table for coverage standard errors.
